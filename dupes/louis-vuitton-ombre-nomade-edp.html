Every note of Ombre Nomade is here — oud, incense, rose, raspberry, saffron, birch, amberwood, benzoin and geranium — and Fragrantica names the target on the product's own page. Owners do not hedge. One calls it 98% similar and a one-to-one dupe. Another, who had worn the Louis Vuitton and could not justify the price, puts it at 95% and says the only thing missing is the raspberry, which they did not want anyway. Read the dissent, because there is some: one reviewer bought it precisely because it did NOT smell like Ombre Nomade to them, describing it instead as a wood-spice-incense aromatic without the rose-raspberry sweetness. That is a minority view but a specific one, and it suggests the match may vary by batch or by skin. A correction worth stating: this fragrance sat on our YSL Y page until August 2026, filed with YSL Y's own note list grafted onto it. The notes above are Fragrantica's, and this is the right page.

#2French Avenue The Shadow Extrait de Parfum

90% match  ·  $40  ·  Oriental Woody
Top Rose, Raspberry, Geranium
Heart Saffron, Birch, Amberwood
Base Agarwood (Oud), Benzoin

Shares with the original: Rose, Raspberry, Geranium, Saffron, Birch, Amberwood, Agarwood (Oud), Benzoin

Eight notes, and all eight are Ombre Nomade's — only the incense is missing. Parfumo calls it a very strong alternative that comes surprisingly close, with a slightly softer leather note that makes it read a little more unisex. The reference we find most convincing is an offhand one: a Fragrantica reviewer writing about a different French Avenue fragrance mentions in passing that his bottle of The Shadow is the Ombre Nomade clone, which is the kind of unprompted aside that is harder to manufacture than a headline. Performance is where the sources genuinely conflict, so here is both. Retailer copy and search summaries claim beast-mode longevity of eight to ten hours or more. An owner who has this alongside Lattafa Al Qiam Gold says the opposite, and puts it bluntly: strange that The Shadow is an extrait and yet not beast mode at all. That same owner is useful on character too — he finds this the more complex of the two, a middle-eastern inky oud that dominates and is harder to wear, where the Lattafa is more leathery and raspberry-forward. If you want the literal Ombre Nomade shape, this is closer. If you want something easier to wear, it is not. One note on finding it: retailers usually list this under Fragrance World rather than French Avenue — the two are the same house, and the bottle says FA Paris.

Sources: Fragrantica published pyramid · Parfumo · unprompted Fragrantica reference · comparative owner review against Al Qiam Gold

#3Lattafa Al Qiam Gold EDP

88% match  ·  $35  ·  Oriental Woody
Top Raspberry, Saffron
Heart Leather, Patchouli
Base Agarwood (Oud), Amberwood, Benzoin, Guaiac Wood, Vetiver

Shares with the original: Raspberry, Saffron, Agarwood (Oud), Amberwood, Benzoin

An owner who wore it to the end of the bottle puts it at 95% of Ombre Nomade after four months of home maceration, with ten-plus hours and sillage beyond eight feet. That maceration detail matters: several reviewers report it opens harsh and settles considerably. Five notes shared, and the raspberry-saffron-oud spine is the right one. What it drops is why this sits below Jean Lowe Noir: no rose, no incense, no birch, so the smoky-floral haze that defines the original is largely absent, and leather and patchouli stand in for it. A reader who has smelled both describes the difference usefully — this is more leathery, more oudy and very raspberry-forward, where the more literal clones lean inky and rose. One dissenting owner finds it harsh and questions what it is supposed to dupe at all.
